## Course goal

This learner guide helps you practise the role of a Lean Six Sigma Yellow Belt. You will learn how to support improvement projects, create process maps, collect basic data, participate as a team member or subject matter expert, and run smaller PDCA improvement activities under guidance.

## CSSC Yellow Belt role context

The Council for Six Sigma Certification describes a Certified Lean Six Sigma Yellow Belt as someone with basic Six Sigma knowledge who does not normally lead major projects independently. Yellow Belts commonly support project teams, act as subject matter experts, develop process maps, and may run smaller process improvement projects using PDCA.

CSSC also states that the Yellow Belt certification has no project requirement and no prerequisites. Certification pathways may include a standard comprehensive exam or a self-paced chapter exam route, depending on the candidate's preference and preparation path.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the labs, you should be able to:

1. Explain the Yellow Belt role and how it differs from Green Belt and Black Belt responsibilities.
2. Describe common certification path expectations and prepare a personal study plan.
3. Explain Lean, Six Sigma, PDCA, DMAIC, waste, variation, and customer value in simple language.
4. Create SIPOC and process maps that help an improvement team understand a process.
5. Write a small PDCA improvement charter with a clear problem, goal, scope, and expected benefit.
6. Support DMAIC Define activities with problem statements, scope boundaries, stakeholders, and VOC notes.
7. Build check sheets, collect simple operational data, and define useful KPIs.
8. Create Pareto charts, run charts, and basic performance metrics such as yield, DPU, and DPMO.
9. Use 5 Whys, fishbone diagrams, and evidence checks to support root cause analysis.
10. Recommend basic countermeasures using 5S, mistake proofing, visual management, standard work, and control plans.

## Case study used throughout the labs

You are a Yellow Belt team member for **Contoso Service Desk**, a support team that handles employee IT requests.

Current problems:

- Ticket handoffs are unclear.
- Some requests wait too long before assignment.
- Agents use different definitions of "resolved."
- Customers complain about repeated status follow-ups.
- Data is available, but it is inconsistent.
- The manager wants a small improvement that can be tested through PDCA before escalating to a larger DMAIC project.

You may use this case study or replace it with a real workplace process approved by your trainer.

## How to complete each lab

1. Read the scenario and objective.
2. Use the Contoso case study or your approved workplace process.
3. Complete the worksheet or template in the lab.
4. Keep assumptions visible. Yellow Belt work should be understandable to a project team.
5. Answer the checkpoint questions.
6. Save each deliverable for the final A3 summary.

## Certification preparation guidance

Use the CSSC reference to understand what Yellow Belt certification represents:

- Yellow Belts have basic Lean Six Sigma knowledge.
- Yellow Belts support project teams and may act as subject matter experts.
- Yellow Belts may develop process maps to support improvement projects.
- Yellow Belts may run smaller PDCA improvements.
- There is no project requirement for this level of CSSC certification.
- Preparation should include review of the Yellow Belt body of knowledge and self-study materials.
