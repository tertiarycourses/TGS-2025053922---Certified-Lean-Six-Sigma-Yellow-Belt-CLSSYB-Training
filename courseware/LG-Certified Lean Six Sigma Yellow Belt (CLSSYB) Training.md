## Lab 1: Yellow Belt Role, Certification Paths, and Improvement Scenario

**Primary skill:** Yellow Belt role and readiness

### Objective

Explain the Lean Six Sigma Yellow Belt role, compare certification path expectations, and choose a simple improvement scenario.

### Scenario

Contoso Service Desk wants to reduce delays in assigning support tickets. You are not leading a major Green Belt project, but you are supporting the team by mapping the process, gathering data, and testing a small PDCA improvement.

### Step-by-Step Lab Guide

#### Step 1 - Define the Yellow Belt role

Create a table:
| Responsibility | Yellow Belt contribution |
|----------------|--------------------------|
Include:
- Basic Lean Six Sigma knowledge
- Process map support
- Subject matter expert input
- Data collection support
- PDCA small improvement work
- Team participation

#### Step 2 - Compare certification pathways

Create a comparison:
| Path | Best for | Preparation need |
|------|----------|------------------|
| Standard comprehensive exam | | |
| Self-paced chapter exams | | |
Add notes that CSSC lists no prerequisites and no project requirement for Yellow Belt certification.

#### Step 3 - Select improvement scenario

Choose one:
- Contoso Service Desk ticket assignment delay
- Real workplace process approved by trainer
Write:
- Process name
- Customer
- Pain point
- Expected benefit
- Data available

#### Step 4 - Create readiness checklist

Create:
| Topic | Current confidence 1-5 | Study action |
|-------|------------------------|--------------|
Include Lean, Six Sigma, PDCA, DMAIC, process mapping, basic data, root cause, and control.

### Deliverable

Submit role table, certification path comparison, selected scenario, and readiness checklist.

### Checkpoint Questions

1. Why does a Yellow Belt usually support rather than lead major projects?
2. Why is process mapping an important Yellow Belt contribution?
3. What makes your chosen scenario suitable for a small improvement?

## Lab 2: Lean, Six Sigma, Waste, Voice of Customer, and Value

**Primary skill:** Lean Six Sigma foundations

### Objective

Identify customer value, voice of customer, waste, defects, and variation in a simple process.

### Scenario

Employees complain that IT support tickets are slow and status updates are unclear. The team needs to understand what customers value before choosing improvements.

### Step-by-Step Lab Guide

#### Step 1 - Capture voice of customer

Create a VOC table:
| Customer statement | Need | CTQ requirement |
|--------------------|------|-----------------|
Example: "I do not know when my ticket will be handled" becomes a need for clear status visibility.

#### Step 2 - Identify value-added work

Create a process activity table:
| Activity | Value-added, business-value, or non-value-added | Reason |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------|--------|

#### Step 3 - Conduct a waste walk

Find examples of the eight wastes:
- Defects
- Overproduction
- Waiting
- Non-utilized talent
- Transportation
- Inventory
- Motion
- Extra processing

#### Step 4 - Connect Six Sigma to defects and variation

Define:
- What is a defect in this process?

- What variation is visible?
- What data would prove the issue?

### Deliverable

Submit VOC table, value-added table, waste walk table, and defect/variation notes.

## Lab 3: SIPOC, Process Mapping, Handoffs, and Yellow Belt SME Support

### Objective

### Scenario

The service desk manager asks you to map the ticket assignment process so a Green Belt can decide whether a larger DMAIC project is needed.

#### Step 1 - Build SIPOC

Create:

#### Step 2 - Create detailed process map

Map each step:
| Step | Actor | Activity | System | Handoff |
|------|-------|----------|--------|---------|

#### Step 3 - Mark pain points

On the process map, identify:
- Waiting points
- Rework loops
- Missing decision rules
- Duplicate entry
- Unclear ownership

- What actually happens
- Where work gets stuck

## Lab 7: Pareto, Run Charts, Variation, Yield, DPU, and DPMO

**Primary skill:** Basic analysis and variation

### Objective

Analyze simple process data using Pareto charts, run charts, variation concepts, yield, DPU, and DPMO.

### Scenario

You collected two weeks of service desk data. The team wants to know which defect category matters most and whether assignment time is improving.

### Step-by-Step Lab Guide

#### Step 1 - Build Pareto table

Create:
| Defect category | Count | Percent | Cumulative percent |
|-----------------|-------|---------|--------------------|
Sort categories from highest to lowest count.

#### Step 2 - Create Pareto chart

Using spreadsheet software:
1. Enter defect categories and counts.
2. Sort descending.
3. Add cumulative percent.
4. Create bar chart for counts.
5. Add cumulative percent line if your tool supports it.

#### Step 3 - Create run chart

Create a run chart of daily average assignment time.
Record:
- Baseline average
- Highest point
- Lowest point
- Direction of trend
- Any unusual point

#### Step 4 - Calculate yield, DPU, and DPMO

Use:
``text
Yield = Good Units / Total Units
DPU = Defects / Units
DPMO = (Defects / (Units * Opportunities per Unit)) * 1000000
``

#### Step 5 - Interpret variation

Write notes:
- What variation is normal?
- What variation needs investigation?
- What data is missing?
- What conclusion should not be made yet?

### Deliverable

Submit Pareto table, chart screenshot or notes, run chart, metric calculations, and interpretation notes.

### Checkpoint Questions

1. What does a Pareto chart help prioritize?
2. What does a run chart show over time?
3. Why should you avoid overreacting to one data point?

## Lab 8: Root Cause Analysis with 5 Whys, Fishbone, and Evidence

**Primary skill:** Root cause support

### Objective

Support root cause analysis using 5 Whys, fishbone diagram categories, and evidence checks.

### Scenario

Pareto analysis shows delayed assignment is the largest defect category. The team asks you to help identify possible causes.

### Step-by-Step Lab Guide

#### Step 1 - Write the problem

Use one focused problem:
``text
Ticket assignment is delayed for <percentage> of tickets during <period>, causing <impact>.
``

#### Step 2 - Complete 5 Whys

Create:
| Why level | Answer | Evidence needed |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
Stop when you reach a cause that can be tested or controlled.

#### Step 3 - Create fishbone diagram

Use categories:
- People
- Process
- Policy
- Technology
- Measurement
- Environment
Add possible causes under each.

#### Step 4 - Prioritize likely causes

Create:
| Possible cause | Evidence available | Confidence high/medium/low |
|----------------|--------------------|-----------------------------|

#### Step 5 - Validate before solution

Write what data, observation, interview, or test would confirm each likely cause.

### Deliverable

Submit problem statement, 5 Whys table, fishbone diagram, cause priority table, and validation plan.

### Checkpoint Questions

1. Why should root cause analysis use evidence?
2. What makes a cause actionable?
3. Why should the team avoid jumping straight to solutions?
