# Lab 01 - Yellow Belt Foundations and Project Scope

## Objectives

- Explain Lean, Six Sigma, and DMAIC.
- Describe the Yellow Belt role.
- Select a small improvement project.
- Draft problem, goal, and scope statements.

## Scenario

You have been asked to support an improvement activity in your team. You need to choose a focused process problem and define the work clearly before mapping or measuring.

## Steps

1. Write your own definition of Lean.
2. Write your own definition of Six Sigma.
3. List the five DMAIC phases.
4. Write one practical activity for each DMAIC phase.
5. Describe what a Yellow Belt can support in an improvement project.
6. Select one simple process problem from your workplace or a trainer scenario.
7. Identify the customer affected by the process.
8. Estimate cost of poor quality, such as delay, rework, complaints, overtime, or frustration.
9. Write a problem statement using observable facts.
10. Write a goal statement with a measurable target if available.
11. Define in-scope and out-of-scope boundaries.
12. Identify the sponsor, process owner, and team members.

## Validation

- The project is small enough for Yellow Belt-level support.
- The problem statement avoids assumed causes.
- Scope boundaries are clear.
- Customer impact is described.

## Deliverables

- Lean and Six Sigma definitions.
- DMAIC summary.
- Project scope worksheet.
- Problem and goal statements.
- COPQ notes.

## Review Questions

1. How do Lean and Six Sigma work together?
2. What is the Yellow Belt role in a project?
3. Why should project scope be clear?
4. Why should problem statements avoid assumed causes?
